Vision Memo · For Director Review April 2026

Prime Video that thinks with you,
not just searches for you.

A 2-year vision for Search & Assistant
Dr. Ming Gong · Prepared for review by Ming Ye
TL;DR

Three years from now, Prime Video is a product that thinks with you — not a search box, not a feed. The customer experiences a true entertainment concierge who understands what they mean, remembers what they like, and acts on their behalf across Amazon's ecosystem.

Behind it: an agentic orchestrator as the customer-facing surface, and a Catalog Intelligence Layer as the substrate every Prime Video surface inherits. Both are built in parallel from Year 1, not sequentially. Evaluation infrastructure is the moat that makes both shippable safely.

The team I want to build is small, opinionated, eval-driven, and over-indexed on full-stack ownership — what I call diamond excellence. The long-term industry bet is a structural moat no streaming competitor can build: Prime Video becomes the only entertainment platform in the world where reading a book on Kindle changes what you're recommended to watch.

i. Product Position

Search and Assistant become one experience, not two products.

Today, Prime Video discovery has a known shape: browse drives ~70-75% of plays, search ~20%, recommendations the rest. But search punches above its weight — it is the moment of highest customer intent, and roughly 13% of streaming searches return zero or wrong results[1]. Each one is a churn signal at exactly the wrong moment.

The deeper problem is intent fragmentation. Customers arrive with three distinct intents — and today's surfaces serve only one of them well:

·Three intent buckets, unevenly served

FETCH ("just play The Boys") works fine — title-string match is solved. FIND ("the gentle blue fox cartoon for my daughter") is broken — string-match fails because the title Tumble Leaf doesn't contain the phrase "blue fox." EXPLORE ("Saturday night, ninety minutes, no tears, something we'll both agree on") is unserved entirely — browse rows can't express constraints, and search has no concept of multi-constraint conjunction.

The product position I'm proposing is straightforward: in three years, a customer should not have to know the difference between search and assistant. They should say what they want — by voice, by typing, in any language, with any number of constraints — and the system responds with a confident play in seconds, with the surrounding context a real concierge would give. Search and Assistant collapse into one experience, not two products. The customer-facing surface is conversational; the underlying machinery is agentic.

This is not "a chatbot bolted onto search." This is search and discovery reimagined as a conversation that thinks with you — multi-turn, context-aware, action-capable, grounded in the catalog. Rufus has demonstrated the architectural pattern at the retail node[2]; Prime Video's job is to extend it into the entertainment node — not replicate the commerce node, and not build in isolation.

iii. Technical Challenges & Big Bets

Two engines, one stack.

The architecture below is a seven-layer stack (data flows top-down) plus a cross-cutting Evaluation Moat that gates every layer. Each layer's role and major innovation are annotated inline. Deeper detail at each layer — subsystem internals, training cadence, latency budgets — lives in the technical companion document. Two design choices matter most for this review:

First, two technical lineages compose, not compete. The system uses implicit embeddings (Lineage B — recall, fuzzy match, cross-lingual) and explicit knowledge graphs (Lineage A — precision, constraints, explainability) together. Industrial e-commerce has converged on this pattern over the past five years (AliCoCo / AliCoCo2 / COSMO / MOON3.0)[3]; Prime Video should not re-invent it.

Second, both engines (Agentic + Catalog Intelligence Layer) are built in parallel from Year 1. Foundation work without an agentic consumer can't tell you whether you built the right foundation. We need feedback from a real Y1 conversational MVP to know what to optimize in Y2's fully agentic phase.

·Five big bets — what we invest in

The five bets below are not five parallel projects. They are a system with explicit dependencies: Bet 01 and Bet 02 are the two foundations (one per lineage); Bet 03 is the customer-facing consumer of both; Bet 04 is orthogonal (cross-Amazon scope, not within-PV); Bet 05 is the cross-cutting safety net that gates everything.

01

Multimodal Video Understanding

Frames + subtitles + X-Ray fused into a 256-dim embedding per title. MOON3.0-style reasoner-then-embedder[3]. Solves: semantic match, fuzzy queries, cold-start. The Lineage B foundation.

02

Viewing Intent Knowledge Graph

~50K scenario nodes, ~10M ⟨scenario, predicate, title, constraint⟩ edges. COSMO-LM distilled to 7B for serving on Inferentia2. Solves: EXPLORE constraint-conjunction, family-mode safety as architectural guarantee. The Lineage A foundation.

03

Conversational + Agentic Assistant

Y1: text-based MVP, single-turn ranking with constraints. Y2: full ReAct loop[4], 14 tools across 4 categories (catalog · external · action · memory), constraint-aware actions, session memory. Solves: the customer-facing experience. What the customer sees.

04

Cross-Amazon Entertainment Graph

Federated graph across Kindle, Audible, Music, Twitch, MGM IP. Edges: same-author, same-IP, adapted-from, shared-actor. Consent-gated, off by default. Solves: the structural moat — 5–10× attribution lift per cross-product action. Year 3 capability.

05

Evaluation Infrastructure

Constraint-conditional eval (AliCoCo2-style four-tuples)[3], per-locale judge panels with calibrated correlation, 60-90 day holdout cohort, behavior simulator. ~30% of headcount budget. Solves: making everything else shippable safely. The bet that prevents Fallout S2.

iv. Beyond Product · Organization Bet

Diamond excellence. Versatile multi-hyphenates.

The old org model — specialist hands off to specialist hands off to specialist — is dead in the AI-native era. Every handoff is friction; friction kills velocity; velocity is the entire game now. The team I want to build is small, opinionated, and full-stack from idea to ship. Each engineer sharpens six edges at once, not one to perfection.

Diamond Excellence×AI-Native Tooling=5–10× Velocity+E2E Innovation

Three concrete commitments operationalize this. (i) Hire for the diamond, not the spike — every hire screened on at least 4 of 6 edges; a 10/10 specialist with 2/10s elsewhere doesn't pass. (ii) Velocity rituals over velocity rhetoric — weekly demo, biweekly ship, monthly retrospect; demo means working software, ship means production traffic, retro means changed behavior. (iii) AI-native operating model — every IC runs a full agentic toolchain (Claude Code, Cursor, custom harnesses, eval suites); ICs compound their own capability quarterly. Prompt engineering is a craft, not a parlor trick.

v. Long-Term Moat · Industry Bet

A moat no streaming competitor can structurally build.

Prime Video already has multiple structural moats — Prime bundle economics, MGM IP, Live Sports rights, Fire TV distribution, and the data depth across the Amazon account. Most of those are givens that Search & Assistant inherits, not builds. The moat that this org can specifically build and own is the cross-product AI experience — and it is the one that, three years from now, will define competitive position.

Three years from now, Prime Video is the only entertainment platform in the world where reading a book on Kindle changes what you're recommended to watch — across fifty-plus markets, with consent.

Netflix structurally cannot do this.No reading product. No listening product. One vertical, one signal.
Disney+ structurally cannot do this.Has IP, doesn't have cross-product user behavior. The graph is missing.

This isn't a feature parity race. This is the moat.

·Why this moat compounds — and the others Search & Assistant inherits

  • Prime Bundle Economics PV's content costs amortize across retail GMV, AWS, ads. Netflix must amortize subscription-only. This lets us optimize for customer experience over short-term ARPU — a freedom competitors don't have.
  • Cross-Product Behavior Amazon has order-of-magnitude more user-behavior signals than Netflix (purchase + read + listen + watch). This becomes a recommendation moat the moment it becomes legible to the customer — which is exactly what Search & Assistant ships.
  • Distribution (Fire TV / Echo) PV is first-class on Amazon hardware. Voice-first ambient discovery on Echo + Fire TV is uniquely accessible to us. Year 3 ambient discovery is the conversion of this distribution moat into an AI experience moat.
  • Live Sports + MGM IP Long-tail content moats inherited from Prime Video's parent investments. Search & Assistant doesn't build these — but it's the layer that makes them discoverable at scale across markets and locales.

None of the other moats become customer-legible without an intelligence layer that exposes them. Search & Assistant is that layer. Three years from now, the question isn't whether Prime Video has structural advantages — it already does. The question is whether the customer can feel them. That is what this org is for.
